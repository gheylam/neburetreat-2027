The dates change the shortlist

Late April into the first days of May is still spring in most of these places. Air can feel pleasant for sitting out, but the sea is cool almost everywhere. For a three-night trip this matters more than summer brochure photos suggest: a 4–4.5 hour flight plus a long transfer eats a lot of a long weekend, and some resort strips are not fully awake yet.

Warmest & most reliable Tenerife, Fuerteventura, south Turkey, Tunisia, Agadir. Days about 22–25°C. Sea still only about 18–20°C.
Pleasant, not swim-hot Fuengirola, Mallorca, Salou, Oropesa, Crete. Days about 20–23°C. Sea about 16–18°C.
Mild and changeable Costa Brava, Corfu, Durrës. Days about 17–21°C, cooler nights, more chance of a shower. Sea about 14–17°C.
Least beach-like Sunny Beach, Bulgaria. Often mid-teens to low 20s, Black Sea still cold. Fine for a cheap city-by-the-sea break, weak as a beach week.

What we scored

Your requirements

  • Health and safety — crime, petty theft, nightlife risk, FCDO travel advice, food and water hygiene.
  • Ease of transport — typical London flight time, how close the resort is to the airport, and whether 20–30 people can move as a group without hiring lots of taxis.
  • Beach quality — sand vs pebbles, cleanliness, space, how usable it is at the start of May.
  • Amenities — supermarket, pharmacy, cash machine, chemist, and a walkable strip for snacks and drinks.
  • Hospital access — nearest proper hospital, and what a UK or EU passport holder actually uses there (GHIC/EHIC vs insurance only).
  • Food quality — beyond the hotel buffet: local restaurants, hygiene, and whether the area is known for decent food or purely cheap tourist plates.
  • Climate, 30 Apr–3 May — daytime heat, evening chill, rain risk, and whether the sea is swimable. May averages are used where early-May data is thin.

Extra criteria added for this group

  • Three-night practicality — long flights and 90-minute transfers waste a short trip.
  • Group fit — can one hotel or two neighbouring hotels take 20–30 people, and is the resort mixed-age rather than a stag strip.
  • Resort character — family package, party town, quiet residential, or purpose-built all-inclusive.
  • Insurance and entry — GHIC/EHIC in the EU, no reciprocal cover in Turkey, Tunisia, Morocco or Albania. UK passports are visa-free for short stays in all of these countries, but Turkey needs 150 days of passport validity.
  • English and cards — how easy it is to sort a pharmacy, taxi or bill if someone is unwell.
  • Season opening — some pools, beach bars and even hotel wings are still closed in early May.

Scores are a committee aid, not science. They are based on typical resort character, official UK health advice, and long-term April/May climate averages — not a live quote for a named hotel.

Does the poll list change?

No. Keep the same five: Fuengirola, Costa Brava, East Mallorca, Side or Alanya, and Crete.

Late May does not rescue the parked options. The Canaries, Tunisia, Morocco, Durrës and Sunny Beach were set aside for flight length, hospital cover or resort character, not just a cool sea.

What does change is the weighting

On 30 Apr–3 May, weather is a real reason to lean Fuengirola or Turkey. On 28–31 May, Costa Brava and Mallorca become honest beach holidays, and Turkey’s extra heat is less of a reason to accept no GHIC and a longer flight.

Late May also means more hotels, beach bars and supermarket hours are actually open — useful for 20–30 people.

Healthcare in one page

UK and EU cards work

Spain (including Mallorca and the Canaries), Greece and Bulgaria: a UK GHIC or still-valid EHIC gets medically necessary state care on the same basis as a local. It is not private-hospital cover and it never covers flying someone home. Hotels in tourist areas often call a private clinic first — say you want the public hospital if you are relying on the card.

Emergency number in Spain, Greece, Bulgaria and Turkey: 112.

Insurance only

Turkey, Tunisia, Morocco and Albania have no UK reciprocal cover for ordinary holidaymakers. Tunisia expects payment on the spot. Morocco’s public hospitals are limited. Albania’s FCDO note is the starkest: facilities are very poor outside Tirana, and you should insure for air ambulance.

EU nationals in the group should still carry their own EHIC/GHIC equivalent for Spain and Greece. In Turkey and North Africa they are in the same boat as UK travellers: private insurance.

Suggested next steps for the small committee

Fuengirola won. The live trip details — Sunday arrival, both board prices, and the payment plan — are on the trip info pack. The notes below are the original committee steps that got us there.

  1. Agree the dates first: 30 Apr–3 May (cheaper, cooler sea) or 28–31 May (about £50 more pp, much more like a beach holiday). Then agree whether weather or a short flight / easy hospital is the tie-break.
  2. Send the main group our top coast choices on either date. We will ask for their first choice and second choices.
  3. On the poll, ask two extra questions: “Are you happy with a 4+ hour flight for three nights?” and “Any medical needs we must plan around?”
  4. Once we decide on a coast, Hey will contact Lucy to pick the hotel by who can actually room 20–30 people on the same board basis, not by the prettiest photo.
  5. Before anyone pays a deposit, re-check FCDO travel advice and make GHIC/insurance a condition of coming.
